Build What You Believe In

Software-Engineer  |  Content-Creator

I believe in people

After more than ten years in customer service, I’ve learned how much impact people can have on one another through positive interaction and perspective. A conversation, encouragement, or simply helping someone feel understood can change the direction of their day—and sometimes their life.

Fear of failure can keep talented people from taking the first step toward what they actually want, and I know that feeling because I’ve had to push through that fear myself.

There were times in my life when I needed the same perspective from outside. I wouldn’t be where I am today without the gift of illumination a few great people have bestowed upon me. Meeting the right people can truly transform your outlook, and sometimes that’s all it takes to bloom.

Your digital frontier.

Technology is one of the ways I help others present ideas to the world. I work with people to turn ambitions into digital spaces that represent them authentically.

I listen first, understand what they want to communicate, and translate that into design, functionality, and content. The goal is simple: create something that feels like a genuine extension of the person or brand behind it and in a way that works well.

About Me

I was born on August 16, 1996, and grew up in a small city in Michigan. When I was young, my parents always told me, “Do what makes you happy,” and I took that to heart.

The problem was that, growing up with that mindset, the world felt incredibly open-ended.

I was impulsive—and, in many ways, I still am. I would constantly have great ideas pop into my head and think, That’s it. That’s the dream. But whenever I wasn’t immediately good at something, or didn’t feel naturally gifted in my pursuit, I became discouraged. Eventually, I stopped putting myself out there. I stopped trying new things.

I didn’t pursue many opportunities or have much direction going through school. I was always scattered. I had ideas, sometimes great ones, but I didn’t know how to bring them to life or what I was supposed to do with them. Even when I thought about giving something a real shot, I could usually talk myself out of it before I ever began.

My friends continued moving forward while I focused on enjoying myself and making the most of the place I was convinced was all there was. Pursuing something beyond my immediate happiness and survival seemed almost wasteful. Life was already difficult enough. Why wouldn’t you spend every day like it was your last?

What I failed to understand then was that the people who push themselves every day toward the life they want are treating every day like it could be their last.

Life happens to all of us, and we do our best to make sense of it.

For years, my goals were simple: enough food in my belly, a roof over my head, and good friends to spend my time with. For a while, that was enough—and there is nothing inherently wrong with that.

I continued living that way until I met a few people who saw more in me than I saw in myself, and they refused to let me forget it. I am thankful every day for those people.

When I arrived in California, I was still a kid just trying to get by. I was still hiding inside the comforts I had built to escape the reality of my own anticipated failure. I was still blunting my vision so the mountains in the distance wouldn’t seem quite so worthwhile.

Eventually, I began pushing past the fear of failure and the idea that I needed to be “ready” before I could begin.

I just started walking.

Even if I’m not always taking enormous strides, I make a point to do something every day that moves me toward the life I want to live. I’ve started dreaming again. I’ve started learning again. And this period of my life has become the beginning of the greatest transformation I’ve ever experienced.

Now, I want to share that same sense of possibility with the people I meet.

My career has been something of a birdshot pattern across different paths. I’ve been a dishwasher at several restaurants, a customer service associate in retail and at a car wash, a banquet setter, a convention teardown assistant, a tent cleaner, a babysitter, a bartender, and now a security systems operator.

On the surface, those jobs have almost nothing in common. But underneath them, they all come back to the same thing: people.

More than a decade of working with people has taught me how much potential can exist inside someone without them ever fully realizing it.

Sometimes a person just needs someone willing to listen. Sometimes they need encouragement. Sometimes they need another person to look at them and see something they haven’t learned to see in themselves yet.

People are capable of so much more than they often give themselves credit for. I want to help people recognize that potential whenever I can. There is always more to a person than meets the eye.

So don’t wait until you feel ready. Start walking.
